[ ANTENNA + ROTATOR · ROOFTOP ]

From a 4-person v0 on an open-source rotator to a clean-sheet v1 tracking LEO, then a suitcase-portable v2 for GEO imagery at 1.7 GHz.

BlueSAT is UNSW's project-based space society. The groundstation project exists to build, operate, and improve the RF infrastructure that lets the team communicate with and track satellites. When I joined, v0 was a small four-person effort based on the SatNOGS rotator v3 open-source design — getting an existing system operational with limited resources is harder than it sounds, but we got it working.

v1 was a clean-sheet design. Over two years I grew the team to 19 members across mechanical, RF, electronics, and software, and led us to a working system that successfully tracks LEO satellites and receives weather imagery at 137 MHz. Full system architecture, interface definition, subsystem tradeoffs, and validation planning were all on me.

Before stepping down I directed the start of v2 — a mobile ground station redesigned for suitcase portability, environmental protection, and operation at 1.7 GHz for higher-resolution imagery. Completed the mechanical enclosure design and antenna testing, successfully receiving imagery from GEO satellite GK-2A during validation trials.

/ 01 — APPROACH

Systems first,
hardware second.

Started with architecture: what did each subsystem need to do, where were the interfaces, what were the tradeoffs? RF chain, rotator control, SDR pipeline, ops console, and the mechanical structure all had to talk to each other. Running a 19-person multidisciplinary team meant the systems engineering layer was as important as the hardware.

/ 02 — RESULT

V1 tracking LEO,
v2 on GEO.

V1 shipped as a fully operational groundstation successfully tracking LEO satellites and receiving weather imagery at 137 MHz. V2 mechanical enclosure and antenna completed validation — received imagery from GEO satellite GK-2A at 1.7 GHz during trials. Handed the project to the next lead at the end of 2025.

changed.
